The position
Strategy

Pain is evidence, not the decision.

The request usually arrives already answered: a workflow people dislike, and a tool that could take it over. That the work is painful is worth knowing — it is how a company points at itself. It is not the same as the work being worth changing, and the two get confused because only one of them is visible from inside.

The decision
Five outcomes

Building is one of the things an engagement can conclude.

Leave it

Work done rarely, by people who are good at it, inside a process that is stable. Automating it moves the cost from doing the work to maintaining the automation.

Simplify it

Steps exist because a system once could not do something. Asking whether the step still has to exist removes some of them without software.

Automate it

The work is right and the rules hold. What is missing is that a person is carrying it between systems that do not speak.

Rebuild it

The workflow is not slow because people are slow. The software underneath models something the company stopped doing.

Retire it

The output has no reader. Nobody finds this without following the work to where it ends.

A workflow is worth rebuilding when the cost of the waiting it creates exceeds the cost of the building — inside one budget cycle, a cycle the person approving it will still be accountable for.

That rule can be wrong. When it is, it is usually because the waiting turned out to be tolerable and the rework did not.

The gates
Where we say no

Some work is a poor candidate regardless of how much time it consumes.

01The process is still moving. Automating a workflow that is being redesigned means building it twice.
02The regulation is under revision. A rule that changes is a rebuild when it changes.
03The inputs are unreliable. Automation makes bad inputs travel faster and reach further.
04The judgement has never been made. If nobody has decided what good looks like, there is nothing to encode.
05Nobody owns the outcome. A system without an owner degrades quietly, and the first person to notice is a customer.
Why this is here

A decision to make first is not a workflow to automate. Where that distinction is missed, the software encodes an argument nobody has had.

The output
What a recommendation contains

Not an amount. Amounts belong in the room where the company is discussed.

—The integrations required, and which of them do not exist yet
—Where a person stays in the loop, and at which decision
—Whether any new interface is needed, or the work stays where it is
—What gets retired, if anything
—The observation that would cause us to withdraw it
—What was assumed because it could not be checked in the time available

Those fields make a recommendation inspectable: what must change, what stays, and what would invalidate it.

WorkGraph
The record underneath it

What the decision was made against.

The WorkGraph holds how work moves, who touches it, which systems it crosses, where it waits, and which decisions get made more than once.

It is built from evidence a company already generates rather than from a session where people describe what they believe they do. It remains part of the system after launch.
