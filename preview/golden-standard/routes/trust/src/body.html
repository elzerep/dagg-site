Permission is attached to the action.

Every intervention defines what the system may read, propose or execute; what it must withhold; and who decides a material exception. Access to context does not imply permission to act.

The exception is part of the product.

Evaluation tests the intended path, the material exception and the way back before release. The operating record keeps the intent, approved context, proposed action, boundary, human decision, evidence and resulting state together so the company can inspect what happened.

Keep the operating model governable beyond any one model.

Dagg treats the model as one part of the harness. Company-specific context, tools, permissions, evaluations and records are designed as governable parts of the company's system rather than as intelligence trapped in one prompt or provider.

This is an architecture principle, not a promise of universal portability. Exact model providers, hosting, data residency, retention, encryption, access control, isolation and contractual commitments are established in technical diligence and the agreement.

A release needs a way back.

Each build defines how the capability can be disabled, how work returns to an approved manual or previous path, and who may make that decision. Evaluation of that reversal should be proportionate to the consequence of the action.

Technical diligence should resolve the exact data sources, model and provider choices, roles, environments, retention, access controls, evaluations, monitoring, incident responsibilities and contractual boundaries for the engagement.
