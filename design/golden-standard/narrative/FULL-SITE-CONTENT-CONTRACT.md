## 1. Purpose

This document defines the complete public story before page design resumes. It
contains the actual copy, the job of every section, the primary object each
section must eventually show, and the route or action each section creates.

The order of dependency is binding:

`company story -> page argument -> section copy -> link graph -> visual job -> component -> motion -> implementation`

Copy is not filler for a composition. Images, product artifacts, components and
motion must serve the argument written here. Layout may compress or reveal depth
progressively, but it may not change the meaning, invent a claim or remove a
required narrative step.

## 2. Company story

### Core story

Dagg designs how a company should operate when people set direction, define
requirements, resolve material exceptions and verify outcomes while AI and
software carry more of the execution. We turn that strategic direction into a
working operating model, retain the company context it depends on, and build the
agents or software required to make the change real.

### Emotional story

The performance gap between companies limited by human-speed handoffs and
companies able to operate at machine speed compounds. The voice never threatens
the reader. The site shows the asymmetry, the operating change and a credible
path across it.

### Execution story

Dagg does not stop at a recommendation. WorkGraph retains the company-specific
context, decisions, rules and boundaries behind the change. Dagg Factory turns
the approved intervention into governed agents or software. Operating evidence
can return to the same context, so the next decision does not begin from an
empty page.

### What the site must make the reader conclude

1. Dagg understands where AI is taking the operating model of a company.
2. Dagg has the strategic judgment to decide what should change and what should not.
3. Dagg has a defined technology and delivery system for turning that decision into a working system.
4. Dagg treats context, ownership, permission, evaluation and reversibility as part of the build.
5. Dagg can be trusted with material work because ambition is matched by execution discipline.

### Narrative architecture - one book, several valid entrances

The site has one global story and one local story on every route. The global
story must read like consecutive chapters, while every route must still work as
a complete landing page for a reader who arrives there directly.

| Chapter | The page answers | The page must create next |
|---|---|---|
| Home | What Dagg makes possible and why the transition matters | What should change first? |
| Transformation | How the operating model and division of work should change | How does that decision survive the move into implementation? |
| WorkGraph | How operating reality, decisions, evidence, ownership and boundaries remain usable | How does retained context become a working system? |
| Build | How approved context becomes governed agents or software | Who remains accountable from direction through release? |
| Company | Why strategic judgment, company building and engineering belong in one partner | What is the first bounded way to work together? |
| Assessment | How one material path becomes an evidence-based operating decision | Begin with one material path. |

Trust is a supporting proof chapter reached from Home, WorkGraph, Build and
Company wherever the reader needs to inspect control. Impact is the sourced
evidence volume and remains unavailable until a real proof passes publication
review.

Three tests are binding:

1. **Headline test.** Reading only the H1 and H2 sequence must reveal a coherent
   argument from premise to next move.
2. **Paragraph test.** Every major conclusion gets enough ordinary-language
   explanation for a smart reader with no prior Dagg vocabulary. Record fields,
   tabs and artifact labels may prove the point; they may not carry the story.
3. **Route test.** The close of each primary chapter asks the question answered
   by the next chapter. A direct visitor receives one short orientation bridge,
   not a repetition of the whole Home page.

## 3. Site-wide editorial contract

- Lead with a conclusion, not a category.
- Use one idea per paragraph and one argumentative job per section.
- Prefer `company`, `work`, `decision`, `context`, `owner`, `boundary` and
  `evidence` before graph, orchestration or model vocabulary.
- Name WorkGraph and Dagg Factory early, but only after the business consequence
  they serve is clear.
- Show execution through an inspectable decision, record, permission or result;
  never through self-praise.
- Use urgency through time, handoffs and compounding. Do not use `transform or
  die`, `10x`, `100x`, `1000x`, `fully autonomous` or unsupported superlatives.
- Do not imply current managed operations, customer outcomes, certifications or
  security guarantees without the evidence ledger and required permission.
- Do not mention team size. Do not build a small-team or efficiency story around
  headcount.
- Use `WorkGraph` consistently.

## Transformation 4 - Choose the intervention

**Internal anchor ID**  
`decide`

**Eyebrow**  
The strategic decision

**H2**  
The first decision is what not to automate.

**Intro**  
Every mapped path is tested against five outcomes. Building is one possible
result, never the premise.

### Preserve

Keep the work as it is when the effort is proportionate to its value and human
judgment is the point of the work.

### Simplify

Remove steps, approvals or transfers that no longer protect a material outcome
before encoding the process in software.

### Automate

Let agents execute a stable path when inputs, rules, exceptions and success
criteria can be made explicit.

### Rebuild

Redesign the workflow and its software together when the current system
preserves the wrong operating model.

### Retire

Stop doing work that no longer changes a decision, protects a requirement or
serves the customer.

**Closing line**  
A recommendation is not complete until it names an owner, a boundary and the
evidence that would change it.

## Transformation 5 - Sequence the transition

**Body**

**Transition sequence**

3. Establish the current state and evidence boundary.

# Narrative acceptance test

The content contract fails if any answer is `no`:

1. Can a reader understand Dagg from Home without opening a supporting page?
2. Does Home stop before it becomes a compressed version of every deep page?
3. Does each supporting page answer a different executive question?
4. Is the strategic operating-model decision clear before WorkGraph or Factory
   architecture appears?
5. Are WorkGraph and Factory central without becoming the whole company story?
6. Does execution appear as a decision, context, permission, evaluation, owner
   and way back rather than as a terminal or dashboard aesthetic?
7. Does urgency emerge from compounding handoffs and capability rather than a
   threat or multiplier?
8. Can every claim be classified as P, R, V, C or X?
9. Does every page create a clear next question and route?
10. Can all visual language, components and motion be assigned a specific
    narrative job after the copy is accepted?
