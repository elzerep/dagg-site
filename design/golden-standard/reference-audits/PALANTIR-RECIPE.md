# Palantir reference audit — the Dagg translation recipe

Audit date: 2026-08-30  
Scope: current official Palantir homepage, AIP, Foundry, Ontology, Impact, one Impact case, Information Security, global navigation, and representative mobile states.  
Status: reference evidence, not a recommendation to copy Palantir's visual surface.

## Executive verdict

Palantir's strongest lesson for Dagg is not its black palette, product graphs, or quasi-military visual language. It is its separation of jobs:

1. the homepage makes an institution-scale promise;
2. product pages expose mechanisms and actual interfaces;
3. Ontology/Foundry give the operating model a named architecture;
4. Impact makes customer outcomes a first-class destination;
5. trust is handled with its own evidence-heavy page and portal;
6. every deep page ends in an action matched to the reader's intent.

The strongest Dagg translation is therefore **Palantir's proof architecture with Anthropic's visual humanity**. Dagg should expose WorkGraph, Factory, Operations, outcome evidence, ownership, and controls just as concretely as Palantir does, while rejecting Palantir's network geometry, circuit-board diagrams, black-command-centre treatment, and jargon density.

## 1. Information architecture and navigation

### Palantir's current recipe

The public hierarchy in the main menu is:

- Sovereign AI
  - AIP
  - Foundry
  - Gotham
  - Ontology
  - Apollo
- Offerings
- Impact Studies
- Documentation
- Careers
- Newsroom
- Palantir Explained

The persistent primary CTA is `Get Started`.

Deep product pages add a second, local navigation layer. Foundry exposes `Palantir Foundry`, `About the Ontology`, `Industries`, `Capabilities`, `Learn Foundry`, and `Start Building Now`. Ontology exposes four anchor destinations: `Ontology Overview`, `Ontology Language`, `Ontology Engine`, and `Ontology Toolchain`.

Opening the global menu produces a full-screen editorial mega-menu: product hierarchy in column one, current news in the middle, and Offerings/Impact in the right columns. This simultaneously communicates product breadth, current relevance, and proof, but it is too information-dense for Dagg's present scale.

### Dagg translation

The clearest customer-facing architecture is the operating journey, not a consulting-category taxonomy:

- **WorkGraph** — map hidden work, decide what should change, and preserve company context.
- **Factory** — automate, build agents, and create custom software.
- **Operations** — orchestrate agents and software when the client does not operate the system itself.
- **Impact** — named cases, ventures, and measurable outcomes.
- **Company** — point of view, team, and why Dagg exists.
- **Start an assessment** — persistent primary CTA.

This is a Palantir-derived recommendation that intentionally challenges `Strategy` as a primary navigation item in the current Dagg framework. Strategic judgment should govern the whole narrative and WorkGraph mechanism; a generic `Strategy` page risks making Dagg look like a consultancy with an adjacent tech practice. If retained, it must have a distinct buyer job and artifact, not merely thought leadership.

Avoid a mega-menu at launch. A compact five-item navigation plus one CTA is more credible for Dagg. Deep pages may use one small local anchor navigation when the reader needs to inspect a mechanism.

## 2. Homepage recipe

### Palantir evidence

The homepage is approximately 6,649 px tall at a 1,440 × 900 viewport. Its hero occupies almost the entire initial viewport. The visible H1 is `Sovereign AI Systems for Every Decision`, rendered at about 80 px with a 78 px line-height. It sits over a real product-interface image/video rather than a decorative abstraction.

The page then moves through:

1. current initiatives/offers;
2. a one-sentence institutional claim;
3. `Our Software`, with five named products and one-line jobs;
4. a human/careers beat;
5. extensive customer testimony with exact outcomes;
6. dual conversion: `Request a Demo` and `Start Building`.

### Strength

Palantir makes breadth believable because product names, interface evidence, customer outcomes, and conversion routes all exist in the same system. The visitor never has to infer that Palantir can implement.

### Failure to avoid

The homepage now leans heavily on a dark, command-centre product visual. The promise is institution-scale but emotionally cold. The horizontally cycling initiatives and large testimony inventory create more breadth than story. Dagg should not reproduce the page as a product catalogue.

### Dagg translation

Use the existing nine-section Dagg arc, with these Palantir-derived proof rules:

1. **Hero:** one institution-level promise plus one working mechanism. The Decision Field should reveal a real WorkGraph excerpt, not remain abstract art.
2. **Reversal:** state Dagg's strategic judgment about what should not be automated.
3. **Decision model:** show the five outcomes and why three can lead to no build.
4. **WorkGraph:** name the architecture and expose how evidence, decisions, ownership, and state persist.
5. **Factory:** show Automate and Rebuild as distinct branches.
6. **Operations:** show how governed agents/software continue to run, escalate, and record outcomes.
7. **Trust:** show bounds, human approval, data ownership, reversibility, and the record.
8. **Impact:** show one real case or venture with an evidence chain.
9. **Assessment:** convert to one bounded first engagement.

The Palantir lesson is `promise → named system → visible mechanism → measured proof → matched action`. The Dagg visual treatment should remain warm, tactile, and abstract-first.

## 3. Product and mechanism proof

### AIP

The AIP page is about 14,969 px at desktop. It opens with a full-bleed laptop showing the actual product. Its narrative then says `Go beyond chat. Enterprise Autonomy` and shows a three-state mechanism: `AI App`, `Action-Driven Logic`, and `Automation`.

The strongest artifact is an annotated product interface with three explicit responsibilities:

- AI reviews alerts and proposes resolutions.
- Human operators review and approve AI-suggested resolutions.
- The underlying logic remains inspectable.

Later sections move from workflow building to evaluation, shipping, development, solution libraries, and two distinct conversion routes: build or explore.

**Dagg should translate:** one inspectable operating artifact, explicit AI action, explicit human decision, explicit underlying logic, and a readable record.

**Dagg should avoid:** a 15,000 px product tour, tiny technical metadata, dark maps, and a three-minute forced scroll experience.

### Foundry

Foundry is about 11,770 px at desktop. The hero uses a 783.8 px left title region and a right-side positioning statement, `The Ontology-Powered Operating System for the Modern Enterprise`. The main visual is a layered architecture diagram.

Its narrative sequence is:

1. position the platform;
2. identify Ontology as the heart;
3. explain closed-loop operations;
4. show collaboration and data activation;
5. map the platform across industries;
6. provide cases and quantified impact;
7. add awards and external validation;
8. end in a detailed sales form.

**Dagg should translate:** WorkGraph as the persistent core connecting strategy, build, governance, and operation; capability-to-case adjacency; real implementation language.

**Dagg should avoid:** the architecture plate as emotional hero, an unbounded catalogue of industries, and a long form embedded into the story.

### Ontology

Ontology is about 9,500 px at desktop. It uses a pale lavender hero `#D9D3F5`, near-white surfaces, an accent purple `#967EFF`, and near-black text. The H1 is approximately 100 px. The page gives the mechanism four named levels:

- Overview
- Language
- Engine
- Toolchain

The central definition is unusually complete: the system encodes the enterprise's `data, logic, action, and security` to automate decisions. It then decomposes that claim into specific jobs:

- encode enterprise data;
- capture enterprise logic;
- model enterprise actions;
- govern the human-agent workforce;
- coordinate high-scale reads and writes;
- activate existing infrastructure;
- expose tools to humans and agents;
- retain security and versioning.

Desktop detail sections use measured modular grids: four items in two 602.5 px columns with an approximately 100 px gap; three items in 368.3 px columns with approximately 100 px gaps.

**Dagg should translate:** this conceptual completeness. A WorkGraph page must say, in plain language, what the system holds, what changes it, who can act, how decisions become executable, and how clients retain ownership/control.

**Dagg must not translate:** the layered block, node network, thin connector lines, symmetrical topology, or the lavender/purple network aesthetic. That is exactly the `circuit-board` feeling Christian rejected. Use one asymmetric path, one consequence, and a material Decision Field instead.

## 4. Impact and customer proof

### Impact index

Impact is Palantir's most directly reusable page type. At 1,440 px it uses a 1,365 px content field with 30 px outer margins. Its hero has:

- 783.8 px left headline column;
- 435 px right explanatory column;
- approximately 146 px of breathing space between them;
- 100 px H1 with 115 px line-height;
- 34 px explanatory copy with about 40 px line-height;
- a 1,365 × 766 px media object immediately below.

The proof section does not merely show a logo. Its selected case combines:

1. an attributed executive quote;
2. partner name;
3. operational impact bullets;
4. a real keynote/video artifact;
5. adjacent navigation to further customer evidence.

The Wendy's spotlight, for example, ties `fixed it in five minutes` to concrete order/inventory workflows across 6,500 restaurants and automated resource allocation.

### Case-page pattern

The Tampa General case is about 4,618 px at desktop. It follows a disciplined sequence:

1. customer + Palantir;
2. one outcome-led lead;
3. customer/product video;
4. impact statement;
5. exact metric cluster;
6. explanation of the operating mechanism;
7. conversion.

The metric cluster contains a 28% patient-hold-time reduction, 30% nurse-staffing-ratio improvement, 95% predictive-model accuracy, 83% reduction in placement-management time, and a disaster-response application delivered in less than 24 hours.

### Dagg translation

Create `Impact` as a true top-level destination, but publish no metric or client context without provenance. Each case should use:

1. **Starting condition** — what operational decision/work was failing or hidden.
2. **Mapped reality** — what WorkGraph exposed.
3. **Decision** — automate, rebuild, operate, or do nothing.
4. **Built mechanism** — agent, automation, custom software, or operations model.
5. **Control** — owner, approval, escalation, data boundary, way back.
6. **Outcome** — verified result, timeframe, and attribution.
7. **Boundary** — what the result does not prove or where the method would not apply.

Until customer proof is publishable, use honest process artifacts and Aloi as owned venture proof. Do not disguise illustrative material as client evidence.

## 5. Enterprise trust

Palantir's Information Security page is approximately 5,350 px and visually behaves more like an executive report than a product page. Its hero pairs `InfoSec at Palantir` with a real-world mission statement and a large photographic image. It then states three objectives:

1. make Palantir safer;
2. make customers safer;
3. make the world safer.

Trust proof is separated into tabs for compliance/accreditation, open-source contribution, and penetration testing. The page links to a dedicated Trust Portal and names external standards rather than making vague security claims.

### Dagg translation

Dagg needs a dedicated Trust page linked from Factory, Operations, and the footer. It should expose only true, verifiable controls:

- client data boundary and deployment model;
- client ownership of WorkGraph and built systems;
- least-privilege access and approval points;
- human escalation and stop conditions;
- readable operating history;
- rollback, reversibility, and exit;
- subprocessors and model/data-flow disclosure where applicable;
- incident contact and response path;
- certifications or external audits only when actually held.

Avoid a generic badge wall. Organize trust around buyer questions and link to evidence.

## 6. Layout, typography, and palette

### Measured Palantir system

- Outer desktop content field: approximately 1,365 px at 1,440 px viewport, with 30 px left/right margins.
- Alliance No.2 for display; Alliance No.1 for body/UI.
- Major desktop display sizes: 100/115 px on Impact, Foundry, and Ontology.
- Mid-level product headings: often 50/50 px or 50/normal.
- Editorial supporting copy: 34/40 px.
- Body: approximately 18/25 px.
- Impact split: 783.8 px headline + 435 px support + approximately 146 px gap.
- Foundry mechanism split: 551.3 px media + 551.3 px copy, placed asymmetrically inside the larger field.
- Ontology modular grids: 2 × 602.5 px or 3 × 368.3 px, with about 100 px gaps.
- Core neutral text: around `rgb(30,33,36)` / `#1E2124`.
- Ontology field: `#D9D3F5`; accent: `#967EFF`; lifted pale violet: around `#F0EEFF`; near-white: `#FAFAFA`.

### Dagg translation

Do not copy Palantir's 100 px heading scale. Dagg's binding 48–72 px H1 is more appropriate for conclusion-led readability and a 7/5 system. Borrow Palantir's asymmetry, clear primary/secondary column roles, generous content field, and large artifact scale.

Keep Dagg's warm paper, lift, ink, coral, and sage palette. Palantir validates the use of one page ground plus contained product/trust surfaces; it does not validate a whole Dagg page becoming black or purple.

## 7. Motion and interaction

Observed motion/interaction patterns:

- full-bleed video/image heroes;
- sticky header and deep-page local navigation;
- scroll-staged product narratives;
- tabbed mechanism states;
- annotated product views;
- tabbed customer spotlights;
- carousels for broad customer proof;
- short CSS transition durations, mostly about 125–400 ms in the inspected AIP state;
- approximately 70 elements in that state carried an animation or non-default transition declaration.

The motion is strongest when it changes what the reader understands: AI proposal → human approval → logic/history; product layer → enterprise action; customer quote → mechanism → outcome.

It is weakest when it lengthens the page, hides content until scroll, or turns breadth into a carousel.

### Dagg motion rule from Palantir

Use motion only to expose causal state:

`mapped evidence → contested judgment → selected path → governed action → recorded outcome`

Do not use network pulses, traveling dots, line tracing, node ignition, infinite product cycling, or command-centre parallax. Those are both aesthetically wrong for Dagg and too close to the rejected circuit-board direction.

Reduced-motion compliance could not be proven in this audit because the selected in-app browser did not expose media-feature emulation. The absence of observed breakage is not evidence of compliance. Dagg must be tested separately with reduced motion enabled and must render every resolved state immediately.

## 8. Conversion system

Palantir uses intent-specific conversion:

- homepage: `Request a Demo` or `Start Building`;
- AIP: build or explore;
- Ontology: read docs, talk to sales, or start building;
- Foundry: learn, start building, or submit a detailed sales form;
- Impact cases: submit a full lead form.

The principle is sound; the embedded forms are too long and interrupt the editorial flow.

Dagg should use:

- persistent primary: **Start an assessment**;
- hero secondary: **See the decision model**;
- WorkGraph: **Explore WorkGraph**;
- Factory: **See what gets built**;
- Operations: **See how it runs**;
- Impact: **See the evidence** or a named case;
- Trust: **Review control and ownership**.

Each CTA must route somewhere real. The initial assessment conversion should ask for the minimum viable information and defer detailed qualification to the next step.

## 9. Mobile findings

Palantir is not a mobile standard Dagg should inherit uncritically.

### Confirmed strengths

- The homepage hero re-composes into a centered promise over full-bleed media.
- AIP preserves a strong product silhouette and a clear CTA.
- Body type remains generally readable.

### Confirmed problems

- Home's menu/search controls are about 40 × 40 px, below a 44 px touch-target target.
- Ontology's local anchor items are only about 26.8 px tall and truncate horizontally; the first screen shows only part of the third tab.
- Impact measured `scrollWidth = 383` against a 375 px client width in the 390 px browser surface, with a visible horizontal scrollbar.
- Impact's sticky bottom menu/search/Get Started strip competes with the video and content.
- AIP's metadata is very small and dense even though the hero image itself adapts.

Dagg's mobile acceptance gates are therefore stricter than Palantir's implementation: no document overflow, 44 px targets, no truncated local navigation, no sticky bar over artifacts, and a readable instrument with an explicit focusable scroll region only when panning is genuinely necessary.

## 10. What Dagg should copy, translate, and reject

### Copy as a structural principle

- Product, mechanism, impact, and trust are distinct page jobs.
- Give the operating model a named core.
- Put inspectable product/artifact evidence next to claims.
- Make human approval, logic, and history explicit.
- Give Impact a top-level destination.
- Use local navigation on deep mechanism pages.
- Offer different next actions for executive, technical, and proof-seeking readers.

### Translate into Dagg's own system

- Ontology → WorkGraph as the client-specific context and decision record.
- AIP workflow → Factory and Operations artifact showing action, exception, approval, and outcome.
- Impact Spotlight → one high-integrity case module, not a testimonial carousel.
- Trust Portal logic → a plain-language Dagg Trust page with ownership and reversibility.
- Dark product proof → contained ink artifact inside the warm editorial field.
- Product hero → Decision Field resolving into a real WorkGraph state.

### Reject

- network-node constellations;
- layered architecture slabs as hero emotion;
- connector-line and circuit-board diagrams;
- black/purple command-centre dominance;
- the phrases `Enterprise Autonomy`, `closed-loop operations`, `Ontology-powered`, or similar jargon without immediate plain-language translation;
- 10,000–15,000 px product tours by default;
- repeated 100 px display headings;
- a mega-menu broader than the actual company;
- auto-advancing customer walls;
- tiny metadata and 40 px primary controls;
- giant sales forms inside editorial pages;
- claims of security, scale, or impact without evidence.

## 11. Binding Palantir-derived gates for Claude

Stop or reject the Dagg build if:

1. WorkGraph is represented as nodes and lines rather than one inspectable work path.
2. a mechanism is explained only in copy, with no artifact or state change.
3. AI action, human judgment, governance, ownership, or rollback is implicit.
4. Impact is a logo/testimonial strip without starting condition, mechanism, and outcome.
5. security is decorative copy or an invented badge wall.
6. Operations remains absent from both the story and route architecture.
7. a product page becomes a long sequence of slides instead of one carrier plus varied section rhythm.
8. mobile deep navigation truncates, wraps into an ambiguous sequence, or creates document overflow.
9. a sticky header or CTA covers the product artifact.
10. any page copies Palantir's circuit-board surface instead of its proof architecture.

## 12. Audit step health

1. **Homepage — strong foundation, visually cold.** Clear institution-level promise, real product, product family, customer proof, and dual conversion; breadth competes with narrative.
2. **AIP — strong mechanism proof, overbuilt experience.** Excellent AI/human/logic annotation; excessive length, dark UI density, and forced-scroll feel.
3. **Foundry — credible platform narrative, too expansive.** Clear core architecture and use-case linkage; long catalogue, diagram-led emotion, oversized form.
4. **Ontology — strategically complete, visually wrong for Dagg.** Best conceptual decomposition; exact circuit/network aesthetic Christian rejected.
5. **Impact index — strongest Dagg reference.** Excellent conclusion-led grid and evidence composition; customer inventory becomes heavy.
6. **Tampa case — strong evidence chain, weak final conversion.** Metrics and mechanism are specific; the long embedded form damages the ending.
7. **Information Security — credible trust architecture.** Mission, objectives, external evidence, and portal are clear; credential list is hard to scan.
8. **Mobile — mixed and not a standard to copy.** Strong hero recomposition; undersized controls, truncated local navigation, and confirmed Impact overflow.

## Evidence limits

- Screenshots and computed layout can establish visible hierarchy, spacing, target sizes, overflow, content order, and observed interactive states.
- They cannot prove complete keyboard, screen-reader, contrast, performance, or WCAG compliance.
- Reduced-motion behavior was not emulated and is unverified.
- Background video, canvas, and scroll-trigger implementation details were assessed from visible behavior and DOM structure, not proprietary source code.
