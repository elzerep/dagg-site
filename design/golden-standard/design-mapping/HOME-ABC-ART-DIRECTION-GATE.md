## 1. What is frozen

All three directions use the same complete Home argument, in the same order and
with the same public wording. The current count is approximately 480
default-visible words when the conditional Impact link is omitted. Global
navigation and footer copy are counted separately.

The shared narrative arc is:

`strategic direction -> operating reality -> decision -> retained company context -> build -> governed operation -> measured learning`

The shared headline spine is:

1. Build the company AI makes possible.
2. AI-native changes who executes — and who decides.
3. Start with how the company really works.
4. The decision becomes a working system.
5. Every operating cycle makes the next one better.
6. One partner, from direction to accountable release.

Every direction must also show the same connected execution example: operating
reality, retained decision, governed execution and returned evidence. It is
labelled as a constructed example at every viewport.

The following foundation is identical across A, B and C:

- Paper `#F0EEE6`, Lift `#FAF9F5`, Panel `#E8E6DC`, Ink `#141413`, Ink 2
  `#33332F`, Coral `#D97757`, Coral Deep `#9E4A2E`, Sage `#7C8471`.
- Geist for structure, Newsreader for explanation and selective editorial
  emphasis, JetBrains Mono only for genuine record metadata.
- 12-column field, 1240 px maximum, 7/5 strategic split, 45/55 product split.
- Compact four-pixel control radius, ten-pixel surface radius and fourteen-pixel
  instrument radius. Not every surface becomes a card.
- Coral means accountable judgment; Sage means permission, boundary or stable
  state; Ink means precision.
- Home remains Decision Field primary with restrained Machine Signal support.
  Operational Evidence appears as a coded, inspectable product artifact, not as
  a third generated-image world.
- No initial-viewport autoplay, no looping ambient spectacle and no scroll
  hijacking. Reduced motion shows a complete resolved state immediately.

## 2. The three directions

The difference must survive a grayscale thumbnail. If the three designs can be
confused after removing colour, the gate fails.

| | A — Editorial Cinematic | B — Intelligent Instrument | C — Sovereign Systems Story |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core impression | McKinsey-level judgment meeting Anthropic material intelligence | A living AI-native operating instrument; xAI behaviour without terminal aesthetics | A decisive cinematic manifesto with Palantir-scale conviction and Dagg warmth |
| Dominant gesture | Ink-to-material hero fade; editorial acts with radical scale changes | Responsive hero plus one scroll-driven instrument that changes state as the argument advances | Full-viewport hero, strategic hinge and one wide product-film field |
| Page silhouette | Broad cinematic opening, short light thesis, one contained dark proof, quiet close | Hero, thesis bridge, tall sticky proof sequence, compact close | Full-screen opening, large sparse hinge, wide dark film act, large consequence close |
| Reader relationship | Directed and authored | Responsive and exploratory | Immersive and declarative |
| Product proof | One elegant connected execution object appears after strategy | The proof object is the central interaction and updates automatically with scroll | One filmic sequence shows retain -> boundary -> build -> evidence return |
| Navigation | Calm editorial flyout; minimal preview | Adaptive flyout with live Build preview; the most advanced of the three | Large, confident overlay with cinematic destination stills |
| Motion | Finite causal entrances at authored scroll thresholds | State-responsive micro-motion and 3-step product resolution | Four-frame product film and strong but finite transitions |
| Primary risk | Becoming a beautiful consultancy page | Becoming a dashboard or terminal | Becoming militaristic, theatrical or too dark |

### A — Editorial Cinematic

The PDF cover is the starting grammar, not a literal slide template. The hero is
one overlapping composition: stable Ink field on the left, a large tactile
Decision Field image on the right and a long optical fade between them. Copy
sits fully on stable Ink; the image is full-bleed, never a card.

After the hero, Paper carries a short strategic argument with one deliberate
asymmetry. WorkGraph and Dagg Factory then enter as two named capabilities inside
one 520–600 px connected instrument. The final compounding and accountability
acts are editorial and quiet, not another feature grid.

Motion is staged rather than interactive: six to eight seconds of finite causal
resolution at meaningful scroll arrival, then stillness. The product proof may
change one state. Mobile uses a separately composed portrait hero with its own
Ink-to-image fade; it is not a stacked desktop grid.

### B — Intelligent Instrument

This direction must feel like an intelligent operating system rather than a
technology landing page. It borrows xAI's adaptive behaviour — responsive
navigation, content-aware previews and product proof that reacts to the reader —
without borrowing terminals, neon, code wallpaper or dashboard density.

The hero occupies 92–100svh on desktop. Decision Field imagery bleeds past the
right and lower edges rather than sitting inside a frame. After the strategic
bridge, the page becomes a 45/55 sequence: prose moves on the left while one
physical proof instrument remains on the right and resolves through WorkGraph,
Dagg Factory, permission and evidence-return states.

The instrument changes automatically as the reader scrolls, but never traps the
scroll. Each state transition is short and completes. The Build flyout is the
signature navigation component: destination list on the left, a meaningful live
preview on the right, with the panel resizing to its content. Mobile converts it
to a full-width disclosure and replaces the sticky sequence with ordered,
complete states.

### C — Sovereign Systems Story

This is the most cinematic and consequence-led direction. It borrows Palantir's
full-viewport courage and OpenAI's early product naming, while preserving Dagg's
warm materiality and human judgment. It must never inherit defense imagery,
HUDs, radar, cold blue-purple colour or false operational scale.

The hero is a 100svh Ink-to-material landscape with stable reading space. The
operating-model shift becomes one large strategic hinge. WorkGraph and Dagg
Factory then appear early and feed a single full-width 16:7 product-film field,
not a dashboard. Four authored frames show retained record, boundary and owner,
Factory build and evidence returning.

The close uses one large conclusion and an editorial route index rather than a
tile grid. On mobile and iPad the film field remains visually dominant but is
recomposed to avoid tiny interface details. It may stay pinned briefly, never
lock or snap the reader.

## 3. Act-by-act composition matrix

| Shared story act | A — Editorial Cinematic | B — Intelligent Instrument | C — Sovereign Systems Story |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Position | Ink/material cover composition; image takes 55–60% of width | Edge-bleed image plus restrained finite Trace -> Decide -> Resolve state | Full-viewport material landscape; strongest scale and deepest Ink field |
| 2. Operating shift | Short Paper editorial turn, 7/5 asymmetry | Compact thesis bridge introducing the instrument | Large conclusion-led hinge with sparse supporting prose |
| 3. Map and retain | WorkGraph and Dagg Factory share one connected object | Sticky instrument enters WorkGraph state | Named systems appear as early chapters feeding the film |
| 4. Build and expose | Same object resolves from context to permitted build | Instrument moves through Factory and customer-surface state | Full-width film shows retained record, boundary and build |
| 5. Operate and compound | One contained Ink proof moment; then stillness | Final proof state returns evidence and explains coordination | Film ends with evidence return, followed by a large consequence line |
| 6. Partner and next move | Quiet editorial close and assessment action | Compact conversion close after instrument completion | Strong cinematic close with editorial onward routes |

No direction may turn each act into an equally sized left-copy/right-image row.

## 4. Required Figma frames

Each direction page contains:

1. A complete 1440 px Home frame with all six acts and the complete public copy.
2. An 834 px iPad composition showing hero, navigation, proof and close.
3. A complete 390 px mobile Home frame with intentional recomposition.
4. A hero motion storyboard with start, judgment and resolved states.
5. A proof storyboard showing all four connected execution states.
6. A reduced-motion resolved still.
7. A short annotation strip covering image-language allocation, motion meaning,
   interaction rules and the direction's principal rejection risk.

## 5. Common rejection gates

Reject any direction that:

- looks like the current rejected Home, a deck, design-studio portfolio, generic
  consultancy template or purchased AI theme;
- uses small decorative image cards where the image language should create
  scenography;
- repeats the same left-text/right-image layout through the whole page;
- uses all three image languages inside one section;
- converts Machine Signal into circuit-board lines, particle wallpaper, a
  terminal, fake chat, fake dashboard or node-map aesthetic;
- makes generated forms resemble sperm, eggs, embryos or biological fertilisation;
- asks the reader to click in order to understand the central story;
- hides causal content from keyboard, no-JavaScript or reduced-motion readers;
- merely stacks desktop composition on mobile;
- drops any of the six narrative acts or rewrites the copy to make the layout fit;
- presents the conditional Impact link before an approved proof exists;
- implies a named deployment, provider, managed-operations proof or quantified
  productivity claim that has not passed the publication gate.

## 6. Decision rule

Christian chooses a worldview, not a colour treatment. The gate is ready only
when A, B and C are each credible final directions and each is strong enough to
win. After selection, the chosen direction becomes the source for the revised
component and interaction library. Only then does responsive browser
implementation begin.
