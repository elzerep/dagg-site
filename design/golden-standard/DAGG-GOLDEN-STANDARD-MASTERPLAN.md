## Executive mandate

This plan exists to produce a Dagg website that is at least as considered, modern and credible as Anthropic, OpenAI, Palantir and SpaceXAI, while being unmistakably Dagg.

The site must make an executive feel the strategic stakes, let an operator understand what changes, and give a technical buyer enough concrete evidence to trust that Dagg can build and run the result. It must not require an engineer to decode it. It must not become so abstract that the business disappears.

The core company story is:

> Dagg helps existing companies become AI-native and new companies begin that way. We combine strategic foresight, company-building experience and engineering to redesign how the business should work: what people direct and verify, what agents execute, what software should remain, change or disappear, and which company-specific capabilities must stay owned. We then turn that direction into a production harness, agents, software and, where required, ongoing operations. Models may change; the company’s context, controls and operating learning should compound.

The core technology story is:

> WorkGraph is Dagg’s proprietary context and knowledge layer: a graph-and-retrieval system connecting workflows, content, systems, decisions and domain knowledge across the company. It supports the strategic map, gives the Dagg Factory context while agents and software are built, and can help orchestrate and improve them in operation. Models can be replaced; the company-specific context and learning remain.

The emotional story is:

> The gap between companies that operate at human speed and those that learn and execute at machine speed compounds. Becoming AI-native is not an isolated technology project; it is a continuous redesign of how the company works. Dagg makes that transition real while the business keeps running.

The tone is full force in the stakes and zero pressure in the voice. The site shows the accelerating asymmetry through changed work, shorter feedback loops and operating evidence. It never threatens the reader with irrelevance; the mechanism makes that risk the reader's own conclusion.

## Decision gates and operating gates

The project has three different kinds of gate. They must never be confused:

1. **Operating acceptance.** Codex accepts or rejects copy, components, assets, packages and QA evidence against this plan. Christian is not asked to approve routine work, implementation details or taste micro-decisions.
2. **Direction choice.** Christian is asked only when two or three genuinely different, high-quality directions would create materially different brand or narrative outcomes. The choice is presented as a bounded vertical slice, not as three complete sites. Each option must include real copy, image language, a working product artifact, motion, desktop and mobile. Color swaps or superficial hero variations do not qualify as alternatives.
3. **Publication.** Christian explicitly decides whether the exact production revision may go live.

A direction-choice gate is therefore not “may we continue?” Work continues up to the choice, and resumes immediately after it. If one direction is objectively superior against the masterplan and no material trade-off remains, Codex chooses it and continues without creating artificial options.

Default ownership:

- Codex owns research synthesis, IA, copy, design rules, package acceptance, red-team decisions and integration.
- Claude Code owns bounded implementation packages and their evidence.
- Christian owns true direction choices, factual/business corrections and publication.

# Part I · The Dagg product story

## The business system the design must express

The product story has six causal stages:

1. **Set the transformation agenda.** Connect the company’s strategic ambition and competitive reality to what AI can now make possible.
2. **Map operating reality.** Understand how the company actually works: visible workflows as well as hidden handoffs, waiting, exceptions, decisions, duplicated effort, content, systems and undocumented knowledge.
3. **Decide.** Preserve, simplify, automate, rebuild or retire. Technology is downstream of judgment.
4. **Build and harness.** Create governed agents around systems that should stay or custom software where the workflow layer should change. Add the company-specific context, tools, permissions, evaluations and records the intervention actually needs.
5. **Operate.** Let agents and software carry execution wherever justified; people set intent, specify requirements, decide material exceptions and verify outcomes. Hand over with monitoring and ownership, or let Dagg orchestrate the system when the client does not want to build that operating capability.
6. **Evolve and compound.** Use production evidence to change workflows, retire unnecessary steps or software and improve the next decision. Persistent context is retained where it creates value, so the next cycle does not start from zero.

This is not a linear consulting process that ends in a report, and it is not a one-off automation programme. It is a live transition of the operating model: workflow by workflow, system by system, with continuity across strategy, production and operation while the business continues to run.

## Market story and harness architecture

The public hierarchy has three levels:

1. **Outcome:** Dagg helps a company become AI-native by changing how work is decided, executed and improved.
2. **Operating method:** Dagg frames the transformation, maps reality, decides, builds, governs, operates and evolves the result.
3. **Technical mechanism:** Dagg’s harness makes models useful inside a specific company through context, tools, permissions, evaluation, orchestration and operating records. WorkGraph is a core technology in that harness and the persistent context layer connecting strategy, build and operation.

WorkGraph maps workflows and their surrounding content, systems, decisions, exceptions, ownership and state. It is a knowledge graph with retrieval across domains, not merely a process diagram. It becomes increasingly valuable as more of the company is transformed because strategic insight, build context and production evidence can be reused.

Every serious transformation needs durable company context. WorkGraph is therefore central to Dagg’s technology story. What varies is not whether context matters, but how deeply WorkGraph is activated and how quickly it matures. A bounded engineering task may begin before the full harness is established; that must not be confused with WorkGraph being optional to the longer transformation architecture.

The harness is the owned system around replaceable models. Depending on the intervention, it can include:

- model choice and routing by task, risk, latency and cost;
- company data stores, content, process context and proprietary knowledge;
- WorkGraph as the structured operating-context database;
- tools and MCP connections into systems and the Dagg Factory;
- permissions, security boundaries and data-flow controls;
- evaluation, alignment, human approval and exception policy;
- orchestration, monitoring, audit records and rollback;
- feedback from production into the next strategic and operating decision.

The commercial point is not a graph diagram or loyalty to one model. It is that the client’s operational alpha — its data, process knowledge, product insight, decisions and learning — remains usable and governable inside a proprietary Dagg harness that can evolve as models change. WorkGraph is a material part of that edge and must not be hidden or reduced to an implementation detail.

## Two starting points, one transformation thesis

Dagg serves two starting conditions:

### Existing company

The company already has customers, people, workflows, data, software, risk and operating constraints. Dagg diagnoses both visible and hidden work, defines the target operating model and moves toward it progressively. WorkGraph can be especially valuable here because the operating reality already exists and must be made legible before it can be redesigned safely.

### New company or venture

The company can be designed AI-native from the beginning: product, workflows, organizational roles, data architecture, harness and software are built together. There may be little historical operating data on day one, so WorkGraph is seeded from strategic and domain insight and grows as the business begins to operate. The first product can begin while that context layer matures; WorkGraph still becomes part of the company’s durable operating architecture.

Both cases use the same Dagg thesis:

`strategic foresight → operating design → proportionate harness → execution → operation → learning`

The difference is migration versus creation, not Dagg’s underlying competence.

## The four Dagg capabilities the site must prove

1. **Strategic foresight.** Dagg understands where AI capabilities are moving and converts uncertainty into decisions for now, the near horizon and the longer operating model. Future capability is treated as scenarios and design optionality, not unsupported prophecy.
2. **Company and product building.** Dagg brings entrepreneurial experience of products, organizations, customers and execution, not only technology advice.
3. **Harness engineering.** Dagg architects the data, context, tools, model routing, security, governance, evaluations and runtime around AI so the company’s edge is not reduced to whichever model is currently strongest.
4. **Execution and operation.** Dagg turns the direction into agents, software and operating systems, then hands them over or runs them with the client. Strategy without implementation and implementation without strategic direction are both incomplete.

The website must make all four legible before it asks the reader to understand an internal product name.

Binding communication rules:

- Lead with the operating transformation, then make WorkGraph unmistakably central as the technology that preserves and activates company context.
- Introduce WorkGraph early enough to establish a proprietary edge, but only after the reader understands the business outcome it serves.
- Describe it externally as a company-specific operating-context layer or part of the Dagg harness, not merely as an internal database.
- Show how it improves a real decision, build or operating loop; never present it as architecture for architecture’s sake.
- Apply WorkGraph proportionately: every transformation needs durable context, while the amount of graph, retrieval and runtime integration grows with the use case and operating maturity.
- Keep the model replaceable and the company-specific context, controls and learning durable.
- Explain WorkGraph’s input-to-production loop only when useful: it captures strategic and workflow knowledge, the Factory can query that context through governed connections, and operations can write evidence back into it.
- Use **WorkGraph** consistently in working copy. Do not introduce alternate spellings produced by transcription unless Christian explicitly makes a naming decision.

## Audience hierarchy

The homepage serves audiences in this order:

### 1. CEO, board and business owner

Question: Why must we become AI-native, and why Dagg?  
Needs: stakes, direction, ownership, business leverage, credible first step.  
Do not lead with: agents, graph schemas, orchestration details.

### 2. COO and transformation sponsor

Question: Where is the real work and how does change become operational?  
Needs: hidden work, handoffs, prioritization, operating model, adoption, measurable baseline.

### 3. CIO, CTO, CDO and security owner

Question: Can this act safely inside our company and can we own it?  
Needs: context, boundaries, integration, auditability, reversibility, data location, monitoring.

### 4. Domain and operations leaders

Question: Will this understand exceptions and improve the work rather than flatten it?  
Needs: human judgment, escalation, real workflow evidence, no generic automation theater.

The design therefore moves from executive consequence to inspectable mechanism. It never starts with architecture.

## Positioning hierarchy

1. Dagg is the transformation partner for companies moving from human-speed execution to an AI-native operating model.
2. Dagg combines strategic judgment with a current understanding of what AI, agents and software can now make possible.
3. Dagg maps how the company actually operates: the visible workflows and the hidden handoffs, exceptions, decisions, systems and knowledge around them.
4. Strategy chooses among five outcomes, including not building.
5. Dagg applies a proportionate harness around the model: only the context, tools, permissions, evaluations, orchestration and records the intervention needs.
6. WorkGraph provides persistent company context when the transformation benefits from it; it is an enabling mechanism, not the company promise.
7. Factory turns the chosen intervention into production agents or software.
8. Dagg or the client operates the result, with people focused on direction, requirements, material exceptions and verification.
9. Production evidence changes the next workflow, decision and software choice, so transformation compounds rather than restarts.

## Urgency without fearmongering

The site may communicate urgency through asymmetry, time and accumulation:

- machine-speed execution versus organizations limited by human-speed handoffs;
- people carrying routine execution versus people setting intent, defining requirements, resolving material exceptions and verifying outcomes;
- reusable company context versus restarting discovery;
- a company that learns across workflows versus isolated AI pilots;
- governed production versus demos;
- compounding context versus commoditized generic models.

Do not write “transform or die,” “1000x” or an unsupported market threat. Do not claim every human task or workflow should be automated. Show the direction of travel and let the widening performance gap make irrelevance feel like the logical risk.

Recommended stakes line to test:

> A machine works at machine speed. Most companies still move at the speed of the handoff.

## How references are used

Each reference contributes one discipline. No reference is copied as a complete visual style.

The research unit is a representative site system, not one attractive screen. For every reference, sample enough distinct page jobs to understand global navigation and footer, institutional positioning, product explanation, evidence/research, enterprise conversion, deep-page structure and mobile recomposition. The goal is not exhaustive crawling; it is sufficient variation to separate a repeatable design rule from a one-page campaign treatment.

Current sample:

- Anthropic: institutional homepage, Research, Claude product and mobile/menu states.
- OpenAI: homepage, Research, Business and mobile/menu states.
- Palantir: homepage, AIP, Foundry, Ontology, Impact index, one Impact case, Information Security and mobile/menu states.
- xAI/SpaceXAI: homepage, Grok, Build, API, Business and mobile/menu states.
- McKinsey/QuantumBlack: institutional homepage, How We Help Clients and Our Approach.

|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | Warm editorial intelligence | Tactile abstraction, generous air, moral/analytical clarity, sparse image use. |

### Anthropic recipe

Anthropic’s useful pattern is not “beige.” It is a disciplined relationship between editorial copy, conceptual imagery and research authority.

Dagg translation:

- Warm paper is the default reading environment.
- Abstract images explain one idea rather than decorate a section.
- Large fields of space let one argument carry the viewport.
- The human dimension appears as judgment, responsibility and consequence.
- Research-like captions make imagery interpretable.
- Use a conclusion-led, asymmetric hero: one dominant idea and one short explanatory counter-column.
- Let sans carry decisions and structure while serif carries judgment and reflective explanation.
- Use one dark argument-driven pause, not repeated dark “tech” sections.
- Compose mobile as its own editorial sequence; never merely squeeze the desktop grid.

Do not copy:

- collage motifs literally;
- Anthropic’s exact typography or coral distribution;
- a pure research-library structure that hides Dagg’s commercial mechanism.

Measured current reference: Anthropic’s institutional hero is approximately 390 px high below a 68 px navigation, with a roughly 1,285 px content field and a 61/67 px H1. Research uses a roughly 1,271 px field, a dominant 837 × 471 px feature image and sparse editorial metadata. The strongest Dagg lesson is the relationship between tactile idea-object, exact instrument, rules and air, not a literal collage style.

Full Anthropic/OpenAI evidence: [reference-audits/ANTHROPIC-OPENAI-RECIPE.md](reference-audits/ANTHROPIC-OPENAI-RECIPE.md).

### OpenAI recipe

OpenAI’s useful pattern is the ability to place research, products, stories and business proof in one ecosystem without making the homepage read like documentation.

Dagg translation:

- Product names are clear and immediately connected to outcomes.
- Editorial modules can introduce WorkGraph, Factory, Operations and Aloi without equal-weight cards.
- Product proof is shown through recognizable artifacts and current states.
- The site has clear paths for executives, technical buyers and prospective partners.
- Put a working proof in or immediately after the hero; the product should be used or inspected before the institution is exhaustively explained.
- Use a dominant 3/1 proof rhythm where one large artifact is supported by a narrow evidence rail.
- Provide two conversion routes: a bounded low-friction assessment and direct dialogue.
- Keep motion inside product and evidence surfaces, with explicit reduced-motion states.

Do not copy:

- a news portal as the homepage;
- a grid of many equally weighted content cards;
- generic launch imagery without a Dagg mechanism.
- a prompt box that does not perform a real WorkGraph job;
- all-white, all-sans sterility or customer-logo theater without a sourced story.

Measured current reference: OpenAI’s homepage uses approximately 32 px desktop gutters, a roughly 1,201 px container, an 895 px primary feature and a 282 px rail. Its Business page pairs 17/28 px body copy with large product media and buyer-language use-case selection. The homepage proves product before company; the Business site proves product, use case and conversion in one system.

### Palantir recipe

Palantir’s useful pattern is the progression from high-stakes outcome to an inspectable operational model, with trust embedded rather than appended.

# Part III · Dagg design constitution

## Seven design laws

### 1. Warm intelligence, precise mechanism

The surrounding world is tactile, warm and editorial. The mechanism inside it is exact, dark and inspectable.

### 2. Abstract → concrete → proven

An abstract image may create meaning. Within the same viewport or the next, that meaning must become a mechanism, caption or artifact. No metaphor is allowed to float without an operational landing.

### 3. Executive first, engineer available

The primary scan is business consequence. Technical depth appears through progressive disclosure, not as an entrance exam.

### 4. Motion is causality

Movement shows evidence arriving, judgment intervening, action executing, governance locking and a record being written.

### 5. Coral is a decision

Coral appears only where human judgment changes the path. It is not a decorative brand stripe.

### 6. Sage is governance

Sage appears only after a boundary, permission or stable operating state has been introduced.

### 7. Proof before scale

One mechanism that works is stronger than ten cards that claim capabilities.

## Palette

| Token | Value | Role | Accessibility rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#F0EEE6` | Main page ground | Default reading surface. |
| Lift | `#FAF9F5` | Editorial lift | Use for short raised acts, not cards everywhere. |
| Panel | `#E8E6DC` | Material depth | Divisions and contained surfaces. |
| Ink | `#141413` | Precision, instruments, final close | Paper text on ink is 15.9:1. |
| Ink 2 | `#33332F` | Body text | 10.9:1 on paper. |
| Coral | `#D97757` | Human intervention | Graphics/large signals only; 2.69:1 on paper. Never small text. |
| Coral deep | `#9E4A2E` | Accessible action text | 5.21:1 on paper. |
| Sage | `#7C8471` | Governance/status | Not small text on paper; use as surface/boundary or pair with darker ink. |

Whole-page balance is a diagnostic, not a target to optimize section by section:

- paper 55–60%;
- lift/panel 15–20%;
- ink/instruments 15–20%;
- coral and sage signals 6–10%.

## Typography

- Geist: headlines, navigation, CTA and interface.
- Newsreader: leads, explanatory prose, captions and selective editorial emphasis.
- JetBrains Mono: genuine status, metadata and instrument labels only.

| Role | Desktop | Mobile | Rule |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| H1 | 56–72 px | 40–48 px | 11–17 words, max 2.5 desktop lines. |
| H2 | 40–56 px | 32–40 px | 5–11 words, max three desktop lines. |
| H3 | 26–34 px | 24–30 px | Conclusion, not category. |
| Lead | 21–24 px | 19–21 px | 26–42 words. |
| Body | 17–19 px | 17–18 px | 30–60 words per paragraph. |
| Meta/mono | 12–14 px | 12–13 px | Never below 12 px, including SVG. |

Humanity is not serif + beige. It is judgment, language and consequence.

## Grid and width

- Content maximum: 1240 px.
- Twelve columns, 24–32 px column gaps.
- Page gutters: 72 px at 1440+, 48 px at 1024–1439, 24 px at 768–1023, 20 px below 768.
- Default strategic split: 7/5.
- Product-dominant hero may use 45/55.
- Reading measure: 560–620 px.
- Instruments can break the grid; long copy cannot.

## Section rhythm

The page must feel composed like a website, not paginated like a deck.

- One carrier may exceed 80svh.
- No consecutive full-height sections.
- Tallest-to-shortest section ratio at least 2.5×; target approximately 5×.
- One warm ground carries most of the page.
- Dark is contained inside instruments and used as the final full-bleed close.
- Maximum three meaningful ground changes.
- Do not repeat eyebrow + H2 + body + three cards.
- Use punctuations of 240–360 px between larger acts.

## Responsive system

- 7/5 collapses at 900–960 px.
- Mobile is designed, not stacked desktop.
- No document-level horizontal scrolling.
- Product pans live inside labelled, focusable scroll regions.
- Five-step sequences remain ordered: horizontal scroll-snap or vertical, never 3+2.
- Text and artifacts establish the same hierarchy at 320 px.
- Sticky navigation auto-hides or adapts and never crosses incompatible backgrounds.

## Image system: The Decision Field

Signature metaphor: a company is a continuous field of work, dependencies and pressure. A human decision changes it. Engineering stabilizes it. Governance defines the permitted boundary.

Materials:

- uncoated fiber paper;
- graphite channels and powder;
- matte-clay relief;
- thin vellum;
- machine-clean cuts;
- soft raking daylight.

Five states share one camera and material world:

1. Map: latent graphite paths appear.
2. Decide: one coral intervention changes direction.
3. Build: layers register around the decision.
4. Govern: a restrained sage limit locks.
5. Operate: flow continues and the control point remains visible.

Hero art-direction proof:

[image-system/decision-field-hero-v2.png](image-system/decision-field-hero-v2.png)

Forbidden:

- synthetic people and offices;
- stock teams, sticky notes and workshop photography;
- circuits, brains, network constellations and flowchart-as-art;
- neon, cyber gradients, space smoke and chrome;
- unrelated section illustrations;
- direct Anthropic collage imitation;
- abstraction with no adjacent explanation.

## Motion grammar

Core semantic sequence:

`evidence → tension → judgment → action → governed state → record`

| Motion | Timing | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Micro response | 180–240 ms | The interface heard the user. |
| Group/reorder | 280–420 ms | Evidence became structure. |
| Human intervention | 320–480 ms | Coral changes the path. |
| State transition | 650–900 ms | The system enters a new lifecycle state. |
| Ambient material drift | 14–18 s, under 2% amplitude | Optional indication that something is operating. |

Rules:

- one moving focal point per viewport;
- no endless tab autoplay;
- one-pass demonstration requires pause/replay and pauses on focus/hover;
- no fake typing, particles, Ken Burns or decorative parallax;
- reduced motion shows the resolved state instantly;
- no-JS shows all core content.

- Primary CTA, secondary CTA and text link.
- Editorial footer with Trust, legal and contact routes.

### Editorial

- Conclusion headline.
- Strategic lead.
- Punctuation statement.
- Evidence caption.
- Boundary/falsifier note.
- Pull conclusion, not a decorative quote.

### Layout

- Carrier split.
- Short punctuation.
- Product stage.
- Proof split.
- Controlled dark instrument.
- Impact narrative.
- Assessment close.

### Product proof

- Decision continuum.
- WorkGraph lifecycle.
- Hidden-work reveal.
- Factory mode selector: automate / rebuild.
- Operating record.
- Control boundary and escalation path.
- Ownership/way-back rail.

### Conversion

- Assessment CTA.
- Assessment deliverable strip.
- Short inquiry form or mail route.
- Confirmation state.

Every component needs default, hover, focus, active, disabled where relevant, reduced-motion behavior and mobile layout.

# Part IV · Information architecture and page template

## Recommended primary navigation

`Transformation | WorkGraph | Build | Impact | Company | Start an assessment`

Rationale:

- The homepage is the executive memo, not the Transformation page. It establishes Dagg's belief about the transition, the stakes, the complete method and enough mechanism and proof to make the company legible in one pass. It then routes readers into the relevant depth.
- Transformation is the strategic deep dive. It expands the AI-native thesis, future operating model, mapping, the five decisions and continuous change without making strategy look like a detached consulting practice.
- WorkGraph earns a top-level destination because it is the proprietary context layer that crosses Transformation, Build and future operation. That cross-lifecycle role, rather than a claim that it is Dagg's only important technology, justifies the navigation position. The page must explain the business value before graph/RAG detail.
- Build is the primary execution destination and Dagg Factory is its lead mechanism. Its local navigation is **Dagg Factory · Operate · Trust**. Factory can be technically as important as WorkGraph without receiving the same navigation treatment: Factory is the engine of a specific stage, while WorkGraph persists across stages. `Build` is stronger than `Implementation`, which sounds like a consulting phase, and cleaner than promoting `Operate` before it is a standalone commercial destination. The page still makes continuing operation explicit.
- Impact houses Aloi and future permissioned proof.
- Company covers mission, leadership accountability and principles without a team roster.
- Start an assessment is a button and the universal entry point.

Trust is linked from Build and the footer. It becomes a top-level item only if buyer research shows it is a frequent first destination.

## Impact architecture

`Impact` is the evidence destination, not a generic case-study library. It answers: **what became materially different because Dagg was involved?**

The page supports two public proof types inside one destination:

1. **Companies built.** AI-native ventures designed and built from first principles. Aloi is the current named proof. About Insight and future ventures enter only when their names and evidence are public.
2. **Companies transformed.** Existing businesses where Dagg changes workflows, software and the operating model. A case may be named only with permission; otherwise it is either accurately anonymized or withheld.

`Systems operated` is a future proof possibility, not a current public category. Operating responsibility may appear as one field inside a venture or transformation case when it is true and evidenced. Do not show an empty third category or imply that Dagg already has managed-operations proof it does not have.

Every full proof uses the same evidence chain:

1. starting condition;
2. strategic thesis;
3. what changed in the operating model;
4. role of WorkGraph/harness, including when a lighter implementation was used;
5. what the Factory built;
6. who operated it;
7. verified outcome and timeframe;
8. attribution, provenance and boundary.

Aloi does not receive a top-level navigation item and is not promoted as a named feature on the homepage. It receives the lead position inside Impact under **Companies built**, with its own complete proof page when the evidence and copy are ready. The homepage may contain a restrained gateway to Impact after the Dagg mechanism has been explained, but it does not become a portfolio page. The content model is ready for future ventures and transformation cases, while the public site contains no empty cards, invented customer names or “coming soon” proof.

## Phase A · Control the problem

### 1. Freeze the current evidence

### 3. Define success and rejection

Turn the ten golden-standard dimensions and hard QA rules into a scorecard.

### 4. Set revision-safe previewing

Freeze every served HTML, CSS, JS, font and image byte into one immutable in-memory snapshot when the preview server starts. Snapshot acquisition itself must be consistency-safe: two consecutive complete passes must produce identical commit/status, candidate paths, byte counts and snapshot ID before the socket binds; a mismatch triggers a bounded retry and then a clear refusal. Compute a deterministic SHA-256 snapshot ID across the sorted path-and-byte set; expose commit, dirty state and snapshot ID in `/__revision`, response headers and invisible HTML metadata. A running server never reads changed source bytes from disk. Source changes become reviewable only after restart, when a new snapshot ID is produced.  
Output: exact-revision preview.  
Gate: every response in a review session carries one snapshot ID, HTML and assets cannot come from mixed workspace states, and a dirty snapshot remains exactly identifiable even when it does not equal a Git commit.

# Part VII · Claude work-package system

Claude receives one package at a time. Each package has a frozen input, bounded files, required renders and a hard stop. It never receives “build the whole site.”

Copy-ready package template:

[CLAUDE-WORK-PACKAGE-TEMPLATE.md](CLAUDE-WORK-PACKAGE-TEMPLATE.md)

First executable package:

[packages/P0-RECONCILE-AND-PREVIEW.md](packages/P0-RECONCILE-AND-PREVIEW.md)

## Package contract

Every prompt contains:

1. Objective.
2. Final copy verbatim, not a topic to rewrite.
3. Authoritative inputs.
4. Files the package owns and may change.
5. Files and decisions that are immutable.
6. Exact deliverables.
7. Visual/interaction invariants.
8. Required viewports and states.
9. Executable tests and the coverage each test must include.
10. Required rendered evidence and independent visual gate.
11. Stop conditions.
12. “Do not continue past this package.”

Claude's four requested inputs are therefore mandatory: final copy, runnable acceptance tests, owned-file boundaries and explicit invariants. They remove productive ambiguity.

Machine tests are necessary but never sufficient for visual acceptance. The earlier test falsely reported “no small text” because it excluded SVG, while the preview mixed current HTML with cached CSS. No-store headers alone do not solve this: a live-disk server can still return HTML from one workspace moment and CSS from another. Each test contract must therefore state its coverage and prove that the rendered HTML, CSS, JS and assets belong to the same immutable snapshot. A test that celebrates fresh bytes appearing without server restart is a rejection condition, not a pass.

### Package evidence JSON

Each package writes one machine-readable result with at least:

```json
{
  "package": "P#",
  "commit": "full commit hash at snapshot start",
  "dirtyAtCapture": true,
  "snapshotId": "sha256 of the complete served path-and-byte set",
  "assetRevision": "same snapshotId",
  "viewports": [320, 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1440],
  "statesTested": [],
  "scrollWidthMatches": true,
  "minimumTouchTarget": {"width": 44, "height": 44},
  "minimumComputedTextPx": 12,
  "textCoverage": ["HTML", "SVG"],
  "contrastFailures": [],
  "placeholderLinks": 0,
  "consoleErrors": 0,
  "reducedMotion": "passed",
  "noJavaScript": "passed",
  "screenshots": [],
  "deviations": []
}
```

The integration gate rejects JSON whose values are unsupported by the screenshots, DOM snapshot or test coverage. A numeric pass may not overrule a visible design failure.

# Part VIII · No-blocker operating protocol

The project must move today without lowering the bar.

1. Make reversible decisions from this plan rather than asking Christian about minor taste choices.
2. If facts are missing, label the artifact illustrative; never invent provenance.
3. If an external tool is unavailable, use the verified HTML prototype as source of truth and mirror to Figma later.
4. If an image is missing, generate it from the accepted Decision Field grammar; do not insert stock people or an unrelated visual.
5. If two iterations fail for the same reason, return to the last accepted gate and change the approach. Do not tune numbers indefinitely.
6. Separate research, design, implementation and QA so the implementer does not grade its own work.
7. One package, one implementation commit and one exact preview snapshot. Evidence generated before that commit must key itself to the served snapshot ID, not pretend to contain its own future commit hash. A clean post-commit run must reproduce the same snapshot ID when evidence paths are excluded from the served set.
8. Preserve working accepted components while replacing failed sections.
9. Do not publish, deploy or merge to production without Christian.

## Legitimate blockers

Only these justify stopping:

- a factual/legal claim cannot be established and cannot be honestly labelled illustrative;
- an irreversible external action requires Christian’s approval;
- production access or credentials are required;
- Christian must choose between materially different brand positions.

Everything else is an execution problem for the team to solve.
