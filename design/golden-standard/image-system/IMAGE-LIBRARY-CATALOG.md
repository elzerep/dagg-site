## What is and is not a production asset

The 21 images under `direction-a-decision-field/`,
`direction-b-machine-signal/` and `direction-c-operational-evidence/` define a
generative visual grammar. They are references, not a quota and not a set of
interchangeable web illustrations.

An image becomes a production asset only when it has:

- a named narrative job;
- one primary image-language role and, at most, one supporting role;
- a defined page and section;
- an exact copy counterpart;
- separately authored desktop and mobile compositions;
- a finite motion use or an explicit still-only decision;
- a registry entry, verified file hashes and a frozen slot selection.

## Why the current FS5 production bank is intentionally small

FS5 does not require a bitmap for every act. WorkGraph, Factory, the decision
continuum, permission, evidence and the assessment path are inspectable coded
mechanisms. The external customer-edge surface remains exact Build copy in FS5;
its stateful two-surface carrier is assigned to FS6. Turning these jobs into
generated images would make the site feel like slides and weaken the proof.

The current production bank is complete only for the founder-selected Home and
the route-specific FS5 slots frozen in the registry and manifests. The earlier
six P4R6 source cuts are historical comparison evidence, not the current asset
count or placement authority. This is still not the final multi-page Dagg image
library; future assets require a named narrative job, geometry and responsive
pair before generation.

## Rejection discipline

Reject or regenerate any asset that reads as sperm and ovum, a tube, a tunnel,
a circuit board, a railway, generic AI spectacle, a fake interface, a design
studio object without causal meaning, or a decorative collage of all three
image languages.

Selected does not mean universally reusable. Placement remains a joint decision
between narrative, copy, component geometry and rendered context.
