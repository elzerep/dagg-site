# Direction C — Operational Evidence

## Thesis

Operational Evidence turns Dagg's operating logic into physical, inspectable material. Every image shows a real state change: evidence is registered, judgment interrupts, a build is aligned, a boundary holds, or retained context compounds. The objects are abstract, but materially credible. They must feel photographed rather than diagrammed.

This is not a fictional interface, a dashboard, or a visual metaphor for “AI.” It is an evidence-bearing instrument language for a company that maps work, makes decisions, builds operational systems, governs release, and learns from what happens.

## Palette

| Role | Token | Reference | Rule |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Base field | Night ink | `#070808` | Dominant field and negative space |
| Structure | Smoked graphite | `#171919` | Ceramic, glass, carriers, rails |
| Evidence | Paper-white signal | `#F2EFE6` | Observable state, registered material, focal truth |
| Judgment | Restrained coral decision | `#C86D59` | A human decision, exception, or deliberate intervention only |
| Verified state | Sage verified state | `#738773` | A passed boundary, verified condition, or controlled release only |
| Optical support | Warm raking light | `#B99A78` | Illumination only; never a large color field |

Night ink and graphite should occupy roughly 80–90% of an image. Coral and sage are semantic events, not decoration. Paper-white is the highest-contrast signal and should remain scarce enough to preserve hierarchy.

## Still-image rules

1. Show one operational event per image. Do not combine the whole Dagg story into one illustration.
2. Build the scene from brushed black ceramic, smoked optical glass, frosted paper-white strata, matte coral inserts, and translucent sage seals.
3. Use deep negative space, controlled falloff, subtle warm raking light, and a single exact focal plane.
4. Prefer macro, low three-quarter, or shallow top-oblique photography. The object may extend beyond the frame.
5. Let state be legible through physical relationships: alignment, interruption, containment, registration, withholding, or return.
6. Never use coral and sage interchangeably. Coral means judgment or exception; sage means verified state or boundary.
7. Keep surfaces optically sharp and materially plausible. Texture should reveal fabrication, not add visual noise.
8. Do not place explanatory copy inside an image. Meaning is completed by the adjacent headline and caption.
9. Avoid symmetrical “hero gadget” staging. Objects are observed in operation, not presented as consumer hardware.
10. Crops must preserve the active event and leave a clean field for page typography where specified.

## Motion language

`07-motion-storyboard.png` defines a six-beat state transition, not production footage:

1. Evidence enters and registers.
2. A mismatch becomes visible through offset or withheld alignment.
3. A coral judgment element engages.
4. Build layers align around the decision.
5. A sage boundary verifies or stops release.
6. Corrected evidence returns to the retained archive.

Motion should be causal and finite. Use one 6–8 second pass, 650–900 ms for each major state change, short holds at judgment and verification, then stop. No decorative drift, ambient particle field, constant scanning, or infinite autoplay loop. Use subtle parallax only where it reveals material depth. `prefers-reduced-motion` receives the resolved final still with no loss of information.

## Section semantics

- **Hero:** a controlled operational system receives a signal, exposes a decision, and returns a verified state.
- **WorkGraph:** hidden work becomes registered evidence and relationships without becoming a network diagram.
- **Decision:** human judgment is the precise intervention that changes what the system may do.
- **Factory:** software and agents are assembled around observable work, constraints, and release criteria.
- **Governance:** a boundary is an active operating condition, not a security badge.
- **Impact:** retained context makes the next operational record clearer and more useful; compounding is shown as progressive fidelity, never as an unsupported multiplier.

## Provenance and truth boundary

These are AI-generated conceptual bitmap assets. They contain no client data, no live Dagg product interface, no customer outcome, and no representation of a deployed system. They are visual carriers for Dagg's method and should not be captioned as screenshots, case evidence, or operational proof. Any adjacent claim still requires its own evidence and provenance.

## Forbidden

- Text, numbers, logos, fake labels, fake controls, or pseudo-readable UI.
- Dashboards, browser chrome, terminal screens, tables, charts, graphs, arrows, or data-viz conventions.
- Circuit boards, neural networks, node-link diagrams, brains, glowing grids, code rain, holograms, or neon-blue futurism.
- People, anonymous hands, faces, office scenes, stock photography, or staged workshops.
- Particles, floating dots, energy filaments, waves, wisps, or decorative “intelligence” effects.
- Consumer-device glamour, circular portholes, cockpit controls, sci-fi machines, or unexplained gadget spectacle.
- Large beige/paper fields, bright editorial collage, or the warm relief/vellum language of Directions A and B.
- Coral or sage used as general accent colors.
- Motion without a state change, autoplay loops, scroll-jacking, or animation that hides the proof state.
- Claims of live operations, customer outcomes, security assurance, or verified performance implied by the image alone.
