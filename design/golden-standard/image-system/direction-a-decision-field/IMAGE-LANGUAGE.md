# The Decision Field

Direction A turns Dagg's operating logic into an original material world. Company context begins as imperfect evidence; judgment changes its course; governance gives it a boundary; engineering resolves it into a precise system. The images explain ideas through physical transformation, never through generic AI symbolism.

## Palette

Use only the frozen Dagg roles:

- Paper `#F0EEE6` — dominant field and breathing room.
- Lift `#FAF9F5` — light-catching paper and editorial relief.
- Panel `#E8E6DC` — material depth and secondary fiber.
- Ink `#141413` — machine precision, resolved record, final system.
- Coral `#D97757` — one human decision, exception or commitment per image.
- Sage `#7C8471` — boundary, permission, feedback or stable state.

Coral and sage are semantic signals, not decorative balancing colors. Neutrals come from paper, graphite and ink only.

## Still-image rules

1. Every frame is a photographed material event: fiber paper, mineral pigment, powdered graphite, matte clay and translucent vellum.
2. A still must have one dominant idea, one focal intervention and enough quiet field to coexist with copy.
3. Ink becomes cleaner as work becomes more legible or operational. Graphite may remain imperfect where context is still being discovered.
4. Coral appears once, exactly where human judgment changes the state. Sage appears only when a boundary or stable condition exists.
5. Composition varies by story: field, spiral, macro junction, staged transformation, bounded relief and inherited sequence. Do not repeat a single layout template.
6. Light is soft directional daylight with shallow, credible shadows. Surfaces retain fibers, abrasions, pigment dust and small imperfections; they are refined, never sterile.

## Motion grammar

Motion reveals causality rather than decorating the page. The canonical sequence is: hidden graphite trace emerges; vellum aligns; coral intervenes; sage establishes a boundary; ink resolves; the resolved state leaves an inherited imprint for the next cycle. Use 320–480 ms for a human intervention, 480–800 ms for material alignment, and 800–1,200 ms for a full state reveal. Prefer opacity, masked reveal, slight material translation and shadow change; avoid parallax spectacle, elastic easing and endless ambient motion. `prefers-reduced-motion` receives the final still with no loss of meaning.

## Resolution and texture

- Source masters are lossless RGB PNG bitmaps.
- The hero master is `1586 × 992`; the five supporting stills are `1536 × 1024` (3:2); the storyboard is `2172 × 724` (3:1).
- Preserve source masters. Derive responsive AVIF/WebP at build time; do not upscale.
- Recommended crops: 3:2 editorial, 16:10 hero, 4:3 tablet and 4:5 mobile. Protect the coral decision point, the sage boundary and the central material relationship in every crop.
- Do not denoise away paper fiber or graphite. Apply no sharpening halo, gradient overlay or color remap that changes the semantic palette.

## Forbidden

No text baked into imagery. No people, hands, anonymous teams, brains, robots, circuit boards, node-link graphs, dashboards, code rain, neon, cyan glow, holograms, shields, locks, glossy plastic, corporate stock photography or decorative motion. Do not reconstruct or closely imitate another company's artwork. Do not use coral as a brand stripe or sage as a generic background tint.
