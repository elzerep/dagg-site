# Strategy

## H1
Pain is evidence, not the decision.

## Lead
The request usually arrives already answered: a workflow people dislike, and a
tool that could take it over. That the work is painful is worth knowing — it is
how a company points at itself. It is not the same as the work being worth
changing, and the two get confused because only one of them is visible from
inside.

## The decision has five outcomes

An engagement begins with a decision, and building is only one of the things it
can conclude.

**Leave it.** Work done rarely, by people who are good at it, inside a process
that is stable. Automating it moves the cost from doing the work to maintaining
the automation.

**Simplify it.** Steps exist because a system once could not do something.
Asking whether the step still has to exist removes some of them without
software.

**Automate it.** The work is right and the rules hold. What is missing is that
a person is carrying it between systems that do not speak.

**Rebuild it.** The workflow is not slow because people are slow. The software
underneath models something the company stopped doing.

**Retire it.** The output has no reader. Nobody finds this without following
the work to where it ends.

## What decides it

The rule we apply: a workflow is worth rebuilding when the cost of the waiting
it creates exceeds the cost of the building, inside one budget cycle — a cycle
the person approving it will still be accountable for.

That rule can be wrong. When it is, it is usually because the waiting turned
out to be tolerable and the rework did not.

## Where we say no

Some work is a poor candidate regardless of how much time it consumes.

**The process is still moving.** Automating a workflow that is being redesigned
means building it twice.

**The regulation is under revision.** A rule that changes is a rebuild when it
changes.

**The inputs are unreliable.** Automation makes bad inputs travel faster and
reach further.

**The judgement has never been made.** If nobody has decided what good looks
like, there is nothing to encode. That is a decision to make first, not a
workflow to automate.

**Nobody owns the outcome.** A system without an owner degrades quietly, and
the first person to notice is a customer.

## What a recommendation contains

Not an amount. Amounts are specific to a company and belong in the room where
the company is discussed.

What a recommendation names, in every case:

— The integrations required, and which of them do not exist yet
— Where a person stays in the loop, and at which decision
— Whether any new interface is needed, or the work stays where it is
— What gets retired, if anything
— The observation that would cause us to withdraw the recommendation
— What we assumed because it could not be checked in the time available

Those fields make a recommendation inspectable: what must change, what stays,
and what would invalidate it.
