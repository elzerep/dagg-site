Dagg · design language

The rules behind the work, in one page you can look at.

Two grounds, one accent, three type roles. Every value below is measured rather than chosen by eye. Switch the accent bottom-right to compare the two variants on real components.

GroundsLight carries the argument. Night marks a beat.
AccentA hairline, a mark, one field. Never a surface.
TypeGrotesk for structure, serif for a spoken voice, mono for metadata.
Weight400 on light grounds. Never 300.
Ink02

Ink, measured against the light ground

Captions carry content, so they are held to the same bar as body text. Anything below 4.5:1 is a hairline label, not a place to put a sentence.

Accent03

The accent, and what it may carry

This is the decision. Coral is a beautiful field colour and an unusable text colour on ivory — 2.96:1. Dagg's violet holds 6.95:1 and can carry both. The variant exists so the choice is made by looking, not by argument.

Type04

Document scale, not poster scale

A headline at billboard size with three lines under it is a poster: loud, empty, and it reads as having nothing to say. The ratio of headline to caption sits near 6:1 on a single-idea page.

Display
Headline, serif, conclusion-led
Subhead, grotesk
Lead paragraph. Sets up the argument in one or two sentences and then stops.
Body. Weight 400, line-height 1.72, measured to about 62 characters. Generous leading is what makes a light ground readable in a room rather than only on a laptop.
Caption. Carries content, held to 4.9:1, never decoration.
Metadata · mono · widely tracked
Night05
The dark beat

Night marks a beat — an opening, a pivot, a close.

Three or four in fifteen pages, never two adjacent. The aurora gives the black depth without ornament: three slow radials, and nothing else.

Accent on nightLifts to a brighter value so it still carries text.
HairlinesWarm off-white at low alpha, never pure white.
